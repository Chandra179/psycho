    Questions and answers

    What is Psycho?

    Psycho is a free tool that estimates the Big Five personality traits and a few other word measures from text you give it. It counts how often certain kinds of words appear and shows which words produced each score.

    Which languages does it support?

    English only. The word lists, the research weights and the reference texts are all English, so scores for other languages would not mean anything. If a text does not look like English, Psycho tells you instead of showing scores.

    Is my text uploaded or stored?

    No. Everything runs in your browser, and your text is never sent anywhere. Nothing is saved unless you choose to keep a reading on your own device, and you can delete it at any time.

    How accurate is it?

    Not very. In a test on 2,442 essays, the scores matched people's questionnaire answers only a little better than guessing (AUC 0.53 to 0.56, where 0.5 is guessing). Use it for curiosity and self-reflection, not for hiring, health or other decisions about a person.

    What research is it based on?

    The Big Five weights come from Yarkoni (2010), "Personality in 100,000 words". The Cognitive Style measure uses a word list from Pennebaker and colleagues (2014). Counting word groups follows a method called LIWC, and it builds on Pennebaker and King (1999).

    Can I see how a score was calculated?

    Yes. Open Calculation details in the report. It shows each word group, how many words matched, the weight used, and the steps from the first number to the final score.

    Can I save the report?

    Yes. Save the report as one HTML file, or use Save as PDF, which opens your browser's print window.
