    Questions and answers

    What is Psycho?

    Psycho is a free tool that estimates Big Five personality traits and related language measures from text you provide. It counts word patterns against a category dictionary and shows which words and categories produced each score.

    Which languages does it support?

    English only. The word dictionary, the research weights and the reference texts are all English, so scores for other languages would be meaningless. If a text does not look like English, Psycho says so instead of showing scores.

    Is my text uploaded or stored?

    No. The analysis runs entirely in your browser using WebAssembly, and no text is sent anywhere. Nothing is stored unless you choose to keep a reading on your own device, and you can delete it at any time.

    How accurate is it?

    Only modestly. In a test on 2,442 essays, the scores matched questionnaire answers only slightly better than chance (AUC 0.53 to 0.56, where 0.5 is chance). It is meant for curiosity and self-reflection, not for hiring, clinical or other decisions about a person.

    What research is it based on?

    The Big Five weights come from Yarkoni (2010), Personality in 100,000 words. The Cognitive Style measure uses the function-word index from Pennebaker and colleagues (2014). The dictionary approach follows LIWC, and the work builds on Pennebaker and King (1999).

    Can I see how a score was calculated?

    Yes. Open Calculation details in the report to see each matched word category, the number of matching words, the weight applied and the steps from raw score to final score and ranking.

    Can I save the report?

    Yes. Save the report as a single HTML file, or use Save as PDF, which opens your browser's print dialog.
